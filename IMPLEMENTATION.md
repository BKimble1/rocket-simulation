# Implementation guide

How KIMBLE Rocket Engineering is built, and why. For conventions when adding a module, see
[`docs/BUILD_GUIDE.md`](docs/BUILD_GUIDE.md); for what is physically modelled and what is
illustrative, see [`ACCURACY.md`](ACCURACY.md).

## Reference inspected

The FAB / ONE photolithography simulator (`BKimble1/Photolithography-Simulation-Site`, branch
`claude/fab-one-round-four-realism-x1ze0a`, HEAD `e33601d`, the newest branch at build time;
`main` held only an initial commit) was read, not copied wholesale: `docs/ROUND4.md`,
`docs/SCENE_GUIDE.md`, `src/three/stage/{Director.tsx, flights.ts, handover.ts, quality.ts, time.ts}`,
`src/watch/{player.ts, timeline.ts}`, `src/styles/app.css`, `scripts/{shot,fps,record}.mjs` and
`tools/narration/`. Patterns adapted here: one camera authority that renders every location;
transitions that start from the displayed pose; capture-and-dissolve for changes no camera
motion explains; readiness before entering a location (hold the last picture meanwhile);
narration and captions on one clock that holds while the picture is not ready; pure timeline
functions so seeking equals playing; quality tiers with hysteresis that never change timing;
virtual-time capture for continuity checks. The narration pipeline (`tools/narration/`) is
reused with its licences. Nothing wafer-specific was carried over. Its running experience was
not inspected in a browser (only its source and documents).

## Stack

React 19, TypeScript 5.9 (strict), three.js 0.186 (WebGL2 renderer), @react-three/fiber 9,
zustand 5, Vite 8, Vitest 5, Playwright 1.56. No drei, no physics engine, no backend.
WebGPU was considered and not used: every custom shader here is GLSL for the WebGL2 path, and
maintaining two pipelines for a checkbox would have been worse than one tested path.

## One dataset, one part graph

`src/vehicle/spec.ts` holds every dimension, mass, engine value and axial station of the K-1.
The geometry, the trajectory model, the telemetry and the lesson figures read it, so they
cannot disagree (tank lengths are derived from propellant masses and densities; a unit test
checks the volumes). `src/vehicle/parts.ts` gives every principal component a stable id; the
3D model tags each mesh with `userData.part` and `userData.material`, lessons, materials,
phase cards, knowledge checks and mission phases refer to the same ids, and
`src/content/materials/assignments.ts` is the single table of which material each part uses.

## Frames and scale

Frame I is inertial with its origin at Earth's centre and axes equal to the pad's east, up and
south at T-0 (`src/world/frames.ts`). Earth rotates inside it, the Sun direction is fixed, and
the Moon moves on a circular orbit coplanar with a due-east parking orbit (a stated
simplification). Positions are double-precision numbers; the renderer uses a **floating
origin** (the camera) so nothing near the camera has large float32 coordinates, and a
**logarithmic depth buffer** so a 5 cm bolt and the Moon share one depth range without
z-fighting. The world is at true scale: the Earth is 6,371 km in radius and the Moon 384,400 km
away. The orbital map is a separate location at 1 unit = 1 Earth radius with enlarged icons,
labelled as such.

## The mission timeline (source of truth)

`buildMission(id)` computes a **reference trajectory once** when a mission opens: a point-mass
model with a US Standard Atmosphere 1976, drag, thrust with the ambient-pressure term, authored
guidance (gravity turn, linear-tangent upper-stage steering found by shooting), Kepler coasts,
Clohessy-Wiltshire relative motion for rendezvous, a restricted three-body integration for the
lunar flyby, ballistic/lifting entry. Output (`src/timeline/types.ts`): per-body sampled tracks
(position, velocity, attitude, mass), keyframed state channels (throttles, gimbals, tank levels,
deployments, pad states), events, phases, a parallel booster storyline, the Auto-director shot
list, and the presentation map. Everything the scene shows is a **pure function of mission
time** (`src/timeline/sample.ts`: cubic Hermite positions from sampled velocities, slerped
attitudes, piecewise-linear channels), so seeking to any moment reconstructs exactly what
playing to it shows: detached stages, fairing halves, deployed arrays, smoke, captions.

Separated bodies are sampled as their own tracks that start from the stack's exact state at
separation, so nothing teleports, and attached bodies share one pose. Each body's pose is the
pose of the shared **model frame** (origin at the first-stage nozzle exit plane), so a separated
upper stage keeps its geometry exactly where it was.

Three clocks: **mission time** (physics, T-0 = liftoff), **presentation time** (what the
scrubber shows; piecewise-linear to mission time, with accelerated coasts and omitted quiet
intervals stated on screen), and the **stage clock** (wall time minus hidden time; decorative
motion and subsystem demonstrations only).

## The camera authority

`src/director/director.ts` is the only code that moves the camera. Locations: **hangar**
(Explore: damped orbit parameters, framing a part's world-space box, log-space zoom, shortest
azimuth path), **flight** (Auto shot list, Ground, Chase, Onboard, Free, and the Map), **map**.
Every flight framing is a live function of mission time (`src/director/shots.ts`), evaluated
each frame; a change of shot or mode pushes a new blend that eases in over the old one while
**both keep being evaluated**, so a transition interrupted by another continues from what is
displayed and the apparent velocity stays continuous. Framings blend look-at points (not
orientations) so horizons stay level; the orientation is built once from the blend. Taking the
camera (drag, pinch, wheel, arrows) switches to Free starting exactly at the displayed pose;
"Back to guided view" blends back. Shots follow their subject and never alter a trajectory.
Changing location captures the displayed picture and dissolves it over the new location; a
location that is not ready is not entered (the picture holds and the page says so).

Panels never hide the subject: a panel that covers part of the screen registers the area it
hides (`useStageInset`: a sheet spanning the width at the bottom on phones, a tall panel at one
side on desktop and phone-landscape). The stage then moves the projection centre into the free
area with `setViewOffset` (and, for a bottom sheet, widens the view to fit the free height),
eased so opening and closing a panel glides. It changes the projection only, never a framing or
a trajectory, and picking and hotspots follow automatically because they use the same camera.
In the hangar, a move between distant subjects keeps the destination in view (the camera pulls
back while the target travels, then closes in) instead of passing close to empty floor.

## Frame order (no per-frame React state)

`useFrame` priorities: −30 player tick → −20 body sampling → −10 director → 0 scene components
(positions = absolute − origin) → 10 render. The interface polls playback at ≤ 12 Hz.

## Explore

The hangar shows the vehicle on an integration stand and two engine display stands (E-1 and
E-1V at full internal detail: the seven booster engines use a lighter cluster version).
Selecting a part (click/tap, the searchable list, keyboard, a lesson link) frames it and shows a
context indicator of where it sits on the vehicle. Intact / cutaway / exploded views animate
the same objects (a view closes before the next opens). The materials lens recolours by
material and links both ways between parts and the materials index. Demonstrations run on the
stage clock and carry honesty labels (slowed, illustrative flow, section view).

## Mission explorer and Watch

The Mission explorer plays the timeline at 0.5 to 8x, scrubs with chapter ticks and event
markers, shows a phase card (what, why now, parts, forces, what enables the next phase), a few
unit-labelled reference values, camera modes, and the booster/main focus switch (time shared).
"Show the part" pauses the mission (`hold('inspect')`), remembers presentation time, camera
mode and play state, opens the hangar with a "Mission paused at T+…" chip, and "Return to
mission" restores exactly that point.

Watch builds a **film** from the mission's presentation map and the measured narration
(`src/watch/film.ts`): each narration segment starts when its anchor event appears; if speech
would overlap the next anchor, the picture in between is slowed (labelled) so speech never runs
ahead of the picture. The film player (`src/watch/player.ts`) is page-clock driven with the
narration kept within 120 ms; it holds while audio buffers or a scene loads, pauses on a hidden
tab, and runs captions-only when narration is off or unavailable.

## Quality and performance

`src/scene/quality.ts`: three tiers (DPR cap, shadow map size, particle budget, atmosphere
samples, cloud octaves, texture size, geometry detail, optional bloom) chosen from the device
and adjusted from measured frame times with hysteresis (three slow 2-second windows to step
down, eight fast windows and a 30 s cool-down to step up). Tiers never change content or timing.
`?quality=` forces a tier; `?diag=1` shows frame times and draw calls.

## Identity

`tools/brand/build_logo.py` writes the KIMBLE symbol (a K whose upper arm is an ascent arc
turning horizontal: orbit needs sideways speed), lockups, the ONE / FAB wordmark and
`src/brand/logoPaths.ts`; the interface and the decals (painted on canvases, mapped on curved
bands) use the same paths.
